— At home

A bedroom that
ends the day.

Light, layers and the small choices that quiet a room down — none of which require buying much of anything.

03 · At home  ·  4 min read

We sell mattresses, so it would be convenient to tell you the mattress is the whole story. It is not. A good mattress in a room that never signals bedtime is a mattress you lie awake on. Most of what makes a bedroom restful costs nothing and takes an afternoon.

Light, in two halves

Split the room's lighting in two: bright light for getting dressed, and a low warm light for the hour before sleep. One lamp on a bedside table at roughly the height of your shoulder when sitting up, with a warm bulb, will do more for how the room feels than any amount of overhead fitting. Then leave the overhead light off after dinner. Your eyes take the hint long before your mind does.

Layers you can subtract

Kathmandu asks a bedroom to be three different rooms across the year: cold and dry in Poush, warm and wet in Shrawan, mild for months either side. Layering beats a single heavy quilt. A cotton sheet, a light blanket, then a heavier one on top — you take a layer off rather than swapping the whole bed. It also means the layer against you is always the one you wash most.

If you wake up hot at two in the morning, the problem is usually the top layers, not the mattress. Try subtracting one before you replace anything.

Give the floor some room

A bed pushed into a corner makes the room read smaller and makes the bed harder to make. If the space allows it, leave enough floor on both sides to stand and put your shoes on. Where it does not, keep the wall side clear of storage so air can move around the mattress — damp collects wherever the air stops.

What to leave out

  • A desk, if you can help it. Work in a bedroom is hard to put down at night.
  • A mirror facing the bed — a personal preference for some, a distraction for most.
  • Anything with a standby light bright enough to read by.
  • Clutter under the bed. Storage is fine; airflow is better.

Sound, last

Heavy curtains do more for street noise than people expect, and a rug on a tiled or concrete floor takes the hard edge off a room's echo. Between the two you can lose a surprising amount of the road outside. Neither is expensive, and both make the room feel warmer in Poush as a side effect.

Do three of these and the room starts to tell you the day is over. That is the whole job of a bedroom, and it is most of what a good night is made of.
