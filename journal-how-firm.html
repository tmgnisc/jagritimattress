— The basics

How firm is
firm enough?

Firmness is the most argued-about word in the shop, and the least useful one. Here is what to ask instead.

01 · The basics  ·  4 min read

Nobody walks into a showroom asking for a mattress with 32 kg/m³ foam over a 1.9 mm pocket coil. People ask for something firm, or something soft, and mean completely different things by it. Two customers can lie on the same mattress and one will call it hard while the other calls it perfect — because firmness is not a property of the mattress alone. It is what happens when your weight, your shape and your sleeping position meet it.

Start with position, not preference

The position you fall asleep in decides more than anything else. Side sleepers need the mattress to let a shoulder and a hip sink in, or the spine bends at the waist all night. Back sleepers need the small of the back supported, so too much give is the problem rather than too little. Front sleepers, whether we recommend it or not, need the firmest surface of the three — anything soft folds the lower back backwards.

  • Side: medium to medium-soft, with a comfort layer deep enough for the shoulder.
  • Back: medium to firm, with a supportive core that does not dip under the hips.
  • Front: firm, with a thin comfort layer.
  • Changes all night: medium. It is the honest answer, not a cop-out.
If you wake with a sore lower back, the mattress is usually too soft. If you wake with a sore shoulder or numb arm, it is usually too firm. Pain in the morning that eases within an hour is the mattress talking.

Then weight, honestly

A mattress compresses in proportion to the load on it. The same medium build reads soft to someone at 90 kg and firm to someone at 50 kg. If two people of very different builds share a bed, a pocket-spring core matters far more than the firmness label — wrapped coils compress locally, so one side can give without dragging the other down with it.

Give it three weeks

A new mattress will feel wrong for a while, especially if the old one had a dip you had quietly learned to sleep in. Your body is used to that shape. Three weeks is roughly how long it takes to stop noticing the change, and it is why we would rather you take the decision home and think about it than buy something in a hurry.

What to ask in the shop

  • What is the core — foam, Bonnell springs or pocket springs?
  • What is the foam density, and is that the stated figure or the measured one?
  • How thick is the comfort layer above the support core?
  • Is the edge reinforced, and what happens when I sit on the side of it?

Any shop that cannot answer those four is guessing, and so are you. Ours are on every product page, and if a number is missing, ask us — we will go and measure it.

— Short version

If you take one thing

Pick for your sleeping position first, your weight second, and ignore the word “orthopedic” entirely — it is a marketing term, not a standard.
